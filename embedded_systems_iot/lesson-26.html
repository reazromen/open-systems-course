What Happens When an Interrupt Fires?

A timer, UART, or GPIO event sets a pending condition. When masking and priority rules permit, the CPU saves required state and runs the handler. Interrupt latency is delay until handler start; execution time is the duration of the handler. Critical sections, higher-priority events, and bus contention affect response. Keep ISRs bounded: acknowledge the source, capture essential state, and signal deferred work. Some peripherals latch one pending bit, so repeated events may collapse rather than queue.

Practical Lab

Common Pitfall

Do not assume every event is queued or call blocking task APIs from an ISR.

Check Your Understanding

1. What determines when an interrupt runs?

Pending state, masking, priority, and architecture rules.

2. Latency versus execution time?

Latency ends when the handler begins; execution time is its duration.

3. Why keep ISRs short?

To bound interference and defer complex work.

Key Takeaways

  • Use board-specific documentation and SDK-supported interfaces.
  • Capture logs and measurements before changing several variables.
  • Define error handling and safe recovery explicitly.
