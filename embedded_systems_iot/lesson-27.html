ISRs, Deferred Work, and Race Conditions

A Boolean flag can coalesce events; a counter preserves counts up to capacity; a queue preserves ordered records; a ring buffer carries streams. Choose based on what cannot be lost and define overflow behavior. An ISR should use only documented ISR-safe APIs. volatile affects compiler treatment but does not make compound operations atomic or synchronize fields. Buffer handoffs need explicit ownership, lifetime, capacity, and producer/consumer rules.

Check Your Understanding

1. When is a Boolean flag appropriate?

When repeated events may intentionally collapse.

2. Why is volatile insufficient for a counter?

Increment is read-modify-write and can interleave.

3. What must buffer handoff specify?

Ownership, lifetime, capacity, and overflow behavior.

Key Takeaways

  • Use board-specific documentation and SDK-supported interfaces.
  • Capture logs and measurements before changing several variables.
  • Define error handling and safe recovery explicitly.
