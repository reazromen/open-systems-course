Power Budgets, Sleep Modes, and Battery Tests

Estimate energy use from duty cycle and validate battery life with measurements.

Learning Objectives

  • Calculate weighted average current.
  • Identify wake sources.
  • Design repeatable power tests.

Build a power budget

List each operating state, its current draw, and the fraction of time spent in that state. Average current is the time-weighted average across active work, radio activity, idle periods, and sleep; peak current still matters for regulators and batteries. Treat datasheet figures as conditions-dependent specifications, not as measurements of your finished product.

Sleep modes and wake sources

Choose a sleep mode based on required wake latency, retained state, and available wake sources. Radio reconnect, sensor startup, and display refresh can dominate energy use even when the MCU sleeps efficiently. Verify which peripherals and memory domains remain powered in the selected mode.

Measure repeatably

Measure current with a suitable instrument and a documented setup; account for meter burden voltage, sampling limits, and transient peaks. Repeat tests under a defined workload and battery condition. Separate measured values from estimates and record the firmware revision and configuration.

Battery life and safety

Estimate runtime from usable battery capacity and measured average current, then include conversion losses, temperature, aging, and peak-load constraints. Use a protected, correctly specified battery and charger; do not short, overcharge, puncture, or operate a damaged cell. Follow the battery manufacturer's handling instructions.

Practical Lab

  1. Estimate current in active, radio, and sleep states; calculate weighted average current for a duty cycle; compare predicted runtime with a documented battery test plan.

Common Pitfall

Calculating runtime from sleep current alone and ignoring radio activity, retries, regulators, and battery conditions.

Check Your Understanding

1. How estimate average current across states?

Multiply each state's current by its time fraction and sum contributions.

2. What can ruin sleep current?

Unexpected wakeups, active peripherals, timers, logging, or network activity.

3. What should a battery test record?

Workload, current/voltage, temperature, battery details, duration, and instrument setup.

Key Takeaways

  • Calculate weighted average current.
  • Identify wake sources.
  • Design repeatable power tests.
