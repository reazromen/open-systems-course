Sampling, Filtering, and Noise

Understand how sampling rate, aliasing, quantization, and digital filters shape sensor data.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain sampling interval and aliasing.
  • Distinguish noise, bias, and quantization.
  • Choose a filter and describe its latency trade-off.

Sampling is a decision

A continuous signal becomes measurements at particular times. The sampling interval determines nominal sample rate. Signal components above half the sampling rate can appear as misleading lower frequencies unless the input is appropriately bandwidth-limited before sampling.

Noise, bias, quantization

Noise is variation; bias is systematic offset; quantization maps a continuous range into discrete codes. They need different remedies. Averaging can reduce some uncorrelated noise but does not automatically remove bias, clipping, or correlated interference.

Simple filters

A moving average smooths variation but can delay changes and blur sharp events. An exponential filter updates a running estimate with a configurable weight. Stronger smoothing generally reduces noise but reacts more slowly. Choose based on signal dynamics and acceptable response time.

Validate before filtering

Check range, ADC reference and resolution, sample timing, saturation, units, and missing samples before tuning a filter. Log raw and filtered values together. A smooth but wrong reading is not a successful measurement.

Practical Lab

  1. Collect raw sensor or simulated samples at a documented interval.
  2. Compare raw data with a moving-average or exponential filter.
  3. Measure response to a step change and quantify delay.
  4. Document sample rate, filter parameters, missing-sample behavior, and limitations.

Common Pitfall

Do not filter before checking units, clipping, timing, and sensor faults; filtering can hide a real fault.

Check Your Understanding

1. What is aliasing?

Higher-frequency content can appear as a misleading lower frequency after sampling.

2. Does averaging remove bias?

Not generally; systematic offset needs calibration or another correction.

3. What is the cost of stronger smoothing?

Usually slower response and more delay.

Key Takeaways

  • Explain sampling interval and aliasing.
  • Distinguish noise, bias, and quantization.
  • Choose a filter and describe its latency trade-off.
