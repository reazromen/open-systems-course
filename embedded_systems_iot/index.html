Embedded Systems & IoT — From Hardware to Connected Devices

Learn to design, program, test, and troubleshoot devices that interact with the physical world. Start with the hardware and firmware boundary, build a working understanding of C and microcontroller peripherals, then progress to real-time systems, ESP-IDF, network protocols, security, power, and fleet operations.

Roadmap: 12 modules · 60 lessons · five practical projects and troubleshooting labs throughout.

Publication status: Lessons 01–41 are published. Lessons 42–60 remain on the reviewed roadmap and will be released in batches after examples and lab steps are checked.

How to use this course

Each lesson follows Understand → Build → Debug → Extend. You will learn the concept, run a small experiment, diagnose a deliberate failure, and change the implementation. The course avoids teaching APIs as magic recipes: when practical, it explains what the processor, driver, bus, scheduler, or network is doing underneath.

Primary path: ESP32-S3 with ESP-IDF. A browser-based simulator is useful for early experiments; later labs need a compatible physical board and a USB data cable. Exact pin mappings and electrical limits must be checked against the board revision you actually have.

Optional warm-up: Microcontrollers, GPIO, UART, I²C, and SPI, and IoT connectivity in the Open Systems Course give a quick conceptual map. They are helpful but not mandatory.

What you need

  • An ESP32-S3 development board is the primary target; use its exact pinout and datasheet.
  • A USB data cable, breadboard, jumper wires, LEDs with suitable resistors, and a push button.
  • One 3.3 V-compatible digital sensor, preferably with I²C, for later modules.
  • A multimeter is strongly recommended before powering physical circuits. A logic analyzer is optional but useful for UART, I²C, and SPI.
  • ESP-IDF toolchain for firmware labs; simulator-first activities are identified where possible.

Safety rule: Do not assume a microcontroller pin tolerates 5 V. Never connect a motor, relay coil, or other high-current load directly to a GPIO pin.

Course design and source map
