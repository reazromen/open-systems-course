Calibration, Accuracy, and Measurement Uncertainty

Treat sensor output as a measurement with limits, not unquestionable truth.

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish accuracy, precision, resolution, and repeatability.
  • Build a calibration procedure using reference points.
  • Report uncertainty and validity limits honestly.

Different quality dimensions

Resolution is the smallest representable increment; precision describes scatter or repeatability; accuracy describes closeness to a reference. A high-resolution sensor can still be inaccurate. Calibration establishes a relationship under stated conditions; it does not make the device perfect.

Calibration procedure

Use a suitable reference, stable conditions, multiple points across the intended range, and repeated readings. Record sensor identity, firmware version, temperature, reference instrument, raw values, fitted parameters, and date. Do not extrapolate beyond tested points without evidence.

Uncertainty and runtime validity

Reference uncertainty, sensor tolerance, noise, drift, placement, temperature, supply variation, and quantization contribute. Reject implausible values, flag stale samples, and detect disconnects. Preserve raw readings for diagnostics instead of silently clamping abnormal values.

Practical Lab

  1. Choose two or more reference points across a safe range.
  2. Record repeated raw measurements and reference values.
  3. Fit an offset or linear correction, then test against a point not used to fit it.
  4. Define handling for out-of-range, stale, and disconnected readings.

Common Pitfall

Do not call resolution or precision accuracy, and do not claim calibration beyond the conditions actually tested.

Check Your Understanding

1. Can a high-resolution sensor be inaccurate?

Yes. Resolution and accuracy are different properties.

2. Why test a point not used for calibration?

It checks whether the correction generalizes beyond the data used to fit it.

3. Why preserve raw readings?

They help diagnose calibration, conversion, and sensor failures.

Key Takeaways

  • Distinguish accuracy, precision, resolution, and repeatability.
  • Build a calibration procedure using reference points.
  • Report uncertainty and validity limits honestly.
