UART and Serial Diagnostics

UART uses start/stop bits and optional parity without a shared clock. Both ends must agree on baud rate, data bits, parity, and stop bits. For logic-level UART, TX connects to the other RX, RX to TX, and grounds are shared after checking voltage compatibility. RS-232 levels are not MCU UART logic. Drivers may queue data; a successful write does not necessarily mean the last byte has left the pin. Distinguish the console interface from application UART pins.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain UART framing and the role of baud rate, parity, and stop bits.
  • Connect compatible logic-level UART devices with correct TX/RX and ground.
  • Diagnose serial errors while distinguishing UART logic from RS-232 levels.

Practical Reasoning

Capture a known console message; verify pinout and voltage before connecting compatible UART devices; send a short message with matching frame settings; document receive-buffer overflow handling.

Practical Lab

  1. Identify the board’s documented UART pins, voltage levels, and whether they are shared with the console; power off before wiring.
  2. Connect TX to the other device’s RX and RX to TX, with a compatible common ground and voltage levels. Never connect RS-232 voltage levels directly to MCU GPIO.
  3. Configure both ends with matching baud rate, data bits, parity, and stop bits; send a short known message in each direction.
  4. Test a deliberately mismatched baud rate, then restore matching settings and record the observed symptoms.
  5. Expected result: the known message is received correctly with matching framing; if not, check pin mapping, console conflicts, ground, voltage, and buffering.

Common Pitfall

Never connect RS-232 voltage levels directly to MCU GPIO.

Check Your Understanding

1. Why must UART settings match?

Both ends must interpret the same bit timing and frame structure.

2. Why cross TX and RX?

Each transmitter feeds the other receiver.

3. Does successful write guarantee physical transmission?

Not always; bytes may still be queued.

Key Takeaways

  • UART endpoints must agree on framing and compatible voltage levels.
  • Cross TX/RX and verify common ground when required.
  • RS-232 is not the same electrical interface as MCU UART.
