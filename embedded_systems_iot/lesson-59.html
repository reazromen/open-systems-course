Integration Review: Security, Timing, Power, and Tests

Review a device as one system, tracing requirements through implementation, tests, and operational evidence.

Learning Objectives

  • Build a requirement-to-test matrix.
  • assess security and timing risks.
  • present power and reliability evidence..

Requirements traceability

For every requirement, identify implementation location, test method, pass criteria, and evidence. Include normal, boundary, failure, and recovery behavior. Requirements such as “low power” or “secure” need measurable acceptance criteria.

Security review

Review trust boundaries, credentials, certificate validation, update authenticity, local interfaces, debug access, and secret-free logs. Prioritize risks and record accepted residual risks. Do not claim a system is secure merely because TLS is enabled.

Timing, power, and reliability

Measure task latency, worst-case paths, stack margin, reconnect behavior, and current under representative workloads. Record test setup and uncertainty. Distinguish measured evidence from estimates and untested assumptions.

Practical Lab

  1. Create a traceability table linking every requirement to implementation, test, acceptance criterion, and evidence. Review security, timing, power, OTA, and recovery; separate measured results, estimates, and unresolved risks.

Common Pitfall

Using vague requirements and screenshots instead of measurable acceptance criteria and reproducible evidence.

Check Your Understanding

1. What is requirements traceability?

Linking each requirement to implementation, tests, criteria, and evidence.

2. Why distinguish estimates from measurements?

They have different confidence and cannot support the same conclusions.

3. Does enabling TLS prove the system secure?

No; identity, authorization, updates, local interfaces, secrets, and recovery also matter.

Key Takeaways

  • Build a requirement-to-test matrix.
  • assess security and timing risks.
  • present power and reliability evidence..
