Watchdogs, Fault Recovery, and Safe States

A watchdog detects missing progress and may flag or reset the system. Feeding it unconditionally from a timer can hide a deadlocked critical task; define meaningful progress and which tasks must report it. Safe behavior is application-specific: an actuator may need to turn off, while a communications device may continue local operation. Hardware interlocks may be needed. Recovery should include bounded retries, backoff, reset-reason logging, and behavior for repeated reboot loops.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain how watchdogs detect missing progress and how they can be misused.
  • Define application-specific safe states and bounded recovery behavior.
  • Create a fault matrix with detection, retry limits, backoff, and reset evidence.

Core Concepts

A watchdog detects missing progress and may flag or reset the system. Feeding it unconditionally from a timer can hide a deadlocked critical task; define meaningful progress and which tasks must report it. Safe behavior is application-specific: an actuator may need to turn off, while a communications device may continue local operation. Hardware interlocks may be needed. Recovery should include bounded retries, backoff, reset-reason logging, and behavior for repeated reboot loops.

Practical Reasoning

Create a fault table for sensor timeout, network loss, task stall, and invalid configuration; define detection, safe output, retry limit, backoff, and recovery; review watchdog feeding logic.

Practical Lab

  1. List at least four faults such as sensor timeout, network loss, task stall, and invalid configuration.
  2. For each fault, define detection, safe output/state, retry limit, backoff, reset logging, and escalation.
  3. Review watchdog feeding so it reflects meaningful progress by required tasks rather than unconditional timer activity.
  4. Use a simulator, host test, or non-hazardous development setup to exercise each fault; avoid disabling safety interlocks or injecting destructive faults into production hardware.
  5. Expected result: a fault matrix with observable pass criteria and bounded recovery. Identify cases where independent hardware protection is required.

Troubleshooting: If recovery loops or the safe state is unclear, stop the test and revisit product hazards, retry bounds, reset logging, and hardware interlocks.

Common Pitfall

Do not feed watchdogs unconditionally or retry forever without considering physical hazards.

Check Your Understanding

1. Why is unconditional feeding risky?

It may continue while a critical task is deadlocked.

2. What should recovery specify?

Detection, safe state, bounded retries, backoff, and escalation.

3. Can firmware alone guarantee actuator safety?

Not always; hardware interlocks may be needed.

Key Takeaways

  • Feed watchdogs based on meaningful progress.
  • Safe state depends on the physical product and hazard analysis.
  • Retries and recovery must be bounded, observable, and tested.
