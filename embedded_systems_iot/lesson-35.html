Feedback and Control Loops

Distinguish open-loop commands from feedback control and reason about stability, sampling, and actuator limits.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify setpoint, measured output, error, controller, and plant.
  • Compare open-loop and closed-loop control.
  • Recognize saturation, oscillation, and sampling-delay problems.

The loop components

A plant is the system being controlled. A sensor measures its output; a controller compares the measurement with a setpoint and computes an action; an actuator applies the action. Error is the difference between target and measured output. Feedback lets the controller respond to disturbances.

Open-loop versus closed-loop

An open-loop command assumes the plant behaves as expected and does not correct output error. Closed-loop control uses measurements to adjust action. Feedback is not automatically better: poor sensor quality, wrong sign, excessive gain, or delay can destabilize a loop.

Sampling and safe experiments

Digital controllers update at discrete intervals. Sampling too slowly, filtering, and computation add delay. Actuators have limits and dead zones. Start with a simulation or low-risk plant; plot setpoint, measurement, error, and output. Change one parameter at a time and define a safe stop condition.

Practical Lab

  1. Draw a block diagram with setpoint, controller, actuator, plant, sensor, and feedback.
  2. Simulate a first-order system and compare open-loop command with simple feedback.
  3. Plot setpoint, output, and control command; identify overshoot and settling time.
  4. Add output saturation and sensor-failure behavior; specify a safe response.

Expected result: A state/response plot or table comparing open-loop and feedback behavior, including setpoint, output, error, and saturation observations.

Troubleshooting: Check feedback sign, units, sampling interval, actuator limits, and sensor scaling before adjusting controller gains.

Common Pitfall

Do not increase controller gain blindly; delay, noise, and actuator saturation can make a feedback loop unstable.

Check Your Understanding

1. What does feedback use?

Measured output compared with a target to adjust control action.

2. Why can a closed loop oscillate?

Excessive gain, delay, sampling, or plant dynamics can destabilize it.

3. What is actuator saturation?

The requested command exceeds the actuator's physical limit.

Key Takeaways

  • Identify setpoint, measured output, error, controller, and plant.
  • Compare open-loop and closed-loop control.
  • Recognize saturation, oscillation, and sampling-delay problems.
