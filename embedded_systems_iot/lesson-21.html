GPIO Inputs, Pull Resistors, and Debouncing

An undriven input can float. Pull-ups bias a line high and pull-downs low; internal pulls have limitations. A button wired to ground with a pull-up is active-low: high means released, low means pressed. Mechanical contacts bounce and may generate multiple edges per press. Debouncing requires a stable level over an interval or a periodic state machine. An interrupt does not remove bounce; it can signal a worker that validates the state later.

Practical Reasoning

Verify safe GPIO voltage and pull configuration; log transitions with timestamps; implement stable-for-N-milliseconds debounce without blocking delays; test short, long, and repeated presses.

Check Your Understanding

1. What does a pull-up do?

It gives an undriven input a defined high level.

2. Why can one press create multiple edges?

Mechanical contacts bounce.

3. Why debounce without a long delay?

Other firmware work continues while the state is validated.

Key Takeaways

  • Use board-specific documentation and SDK-supported interfaces.
