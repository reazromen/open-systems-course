Driving Actuators Safely

Connect firmware commands to physical loads without exceeding GPIO, driver, thermal, or mechanical limits.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain why GPIO pins cannot directly drive many loads.
  • Select driver and protection based on load characteristics.
  • Define safe startup, shutdown, and fault behavior.

GPIO is a control signal

A GPIO is intended for limited logic-level current, not as a general power source. Motors, relays, solenoids, heaters, and lamps may draw much more current and generate inductive spikes. Use a driver rated for load voltage, current, thermal dissipation, and switching behavior.

Choose the right driver

A transistor or MOSFET, motor driver, relay driver, or dedicated controller may be appropriate. Inductive loads often need a specified flyback path or clamp. Check drive voltage, voltage drop, heat, polarity, current limiting, and isolation requirements. Follow the driver reference circuit.

Power and safe behavior

Load current can cause voltage drops and noise in logic circuits. Verify grounding, supply polarity, and current capacity. Set outputs to safe states during startup and reset; define behavior for sensor failure, communication loss, watchdog reset, and invalid commands. Hardware interlocks may be necessary.

Practical Lab

  1. Choose a low-voltage, current-limited training load and documented driver; do not start with mains voltage.
  2. Draw supply, driver, load, protection, and control connections before wiring.
  3. Verify GPIO limits, driver ratings, load current, and default output state against datasheets.
  4. Test startup, reset, disconnect, and fault paths with the load safely restrained or replaced by an indicator.

Common Pitfall

Never connect a motor, relay coil, or high-current load directly to GPIO; a software pin default is not a complete safety mechanism.

Check Your Understanding

1. Why is a driver needed?

Many loads exceed GPIO limits or generate transients.

2. What must be checked for an inductive load?

Driver ratings and a suitable flyback or clamp path specified for the circuit.

3. Why define a safe startup state?

Boot and reset transitions must not unintentionally energize the load.

Key Takeaways

  • Explain why GPIO pins cannot directly drive many loads.
  • Select driver and protection based on load characteristics.
  • Define safe startup, shutdown, and fault behavior.
